Spectral geometry
STEP 01

A finite spectrum and its band

Fix the matrix parameter and level. The dimension alone moves.

The N×NN\times N KMS matrix has entries a|i−j|a^{|i-j|}. Since BN=TN(a)−τINB_N=T_N(a)-\tau I_N is real symmetric, its smallest singular value measures the distance from τ\tau to the finite spectrum.

The symbol is

μa(θ)=1−a21−2acosθ+a2. \mu_a(\theta)=\frac{1-a^2}{1-2a\cos\theta+a^2}.
μ−=1−a1+a,μ+=1+a1−a. \mu_- = \frac{1-a}{1+a},\qquad \mu_+=\frac{1+a}{1-a}.
The symbol crosses an interior level transversely, touches a band edge quadratically, and stays separated from an exterior level. These are the three spectral mechanisms behind the five conditioning regimes.

STEP 02

Exact phase quantization

The finite eigenangles satisfy an exact phase law, not merely an approximation.

For the eigenangles θk\theta_k there is an exact relation

GN(θk)=Nθk+ψa(θk)=kπ. G_N(\theta_k)=N\theta_k+\psi_a(\theta_k)=k\pi.
GN′(θ)=N+μa(θ). G_N'(\theta)=N+\mu_a(\theta).
At the unique interior crossing angle θτ\theta_\tau, we have μa(θτ)=τ\mu_a(\theta_\tau)=\tau, hence GN′(θτ)=N+τG_N'(\theta_\tau)=N+\tau. This normalization later controls the distance to the nearest eigenvalue.

STEP 03

The Diophantine triangle and determinant

A matrix singularity becomes an exact integer condition.

Set α=θτ/π\alpha=\theta_\tau/\pi and β=1−ψa(θτ)/π\beta=1-\psi_a(\theta_\tau)/\pi. The interior parameter pair lies in the open triangle α>0\alpha>0, β>0\beta>0, α+β<1\alpha+\beta<1, with inverse coordinates

τ=sin(πβ)sin(πα),a=cos(π(α+β)/2)cos(π(α−β)/2). \tau=\frac{\sin(\pi\beta)}{\sin(\pi\alpha)},\qquad a=\frac{\cos\bigl(\pi(\alpha+\beta)/2\bigr)}{\cos\bigl(\pi(\alpha-\beta)/2\bigr)}.
The exact determinant is a nonzero prefactor times sin(π(Nα−β))\sin\!\bigl(\pi(N\alpha-\beta)\bigr). Therefore
detBN=0⇔Nα−β∈ℤ. \det B_N=0\iff N\alpha-\beta\in\mathbb Z.
This is the main change of viewpoint: an NN-dimensional kernel problem is now an inhomogeneous arithmetic rotation problem.

Arithmetic consequences
STEP 04

Zero, one, or infinitely many hits

Rational and irrational phase points produce different recurrence patterns.

Each exact singular dimension lies on a line β=Nα−m\beta=N\alpha-m for some integer mm. If α=p/q\alpha=p/q is rational in lowest terms, compatible values of β\beta yield exactly one residue class of singular dimensions modulo qq; incompatible values yield none.

If α\alpha is irrational, two different singular dimensions would force an integer multiple of α\alpha to be integral, which is impossible. Across parameters, the number of singular dimensions can therefore be 00, 11, or ∞\infty.

STEP 05

Conditioning in five regimes

The near miss is measured by a single scalar distance.

Define the nearest-integer residual dN=dist(Nα−β,ℤ)d_N=\operatorname{dist}(N\alpha-\beta,\mathbb Z). In the interior, for fixed (a,τ)(a,\tau) and sufficiently large nonsingular dimensions,

σmin(BN)=πγ(a,τ)dNN+τ(1+O(N−1)), \sigma_{\min}(B_N)=\pi\gamma(a,\tau)\frac{d_N}{N+\tau}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
κ2(BN)=C(a,τ)N+τdN(1+O(N−1)). \kappa_2(B_N)=C(a,\tau)\frac{N+\tau}{d_N}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr).
Outside the band, conditioning remains bounded; at either edge it grows quadratically with an explicit constant. The interior error estimates are relative and are not uniform when τ\tau approaches a band edge. The proof uses the actual induced ℓ2\ell^2 operator norm.

Limit laws
STEP 06

Rational atoms and irrational bulk laws

Periodic arithmetic produces atoms; equidistribution produces a Pareto bulk.

For rational α\alpha, the normalized conditioning sequence follows a periodic orbit; if one residue class is singular, the extended-real empirical measure has an atom at +∞+\infty.

For irrational α\alpha, the residuals have an equidistributed bulk. Along nonsingular dimensions, the empirical law of κ2(BN)/N\kappa_2(B_N)/N tends to the distribution of C/UC/U, with UU uniform on [0,12][0,\tfrac12] and positive-tail law min(1,2C/t)\min(1,2C/t). This is a result for a deterministic sequence, not an independent random sample; its Cesàro mean diverges.

STEP 07

Exceptional spikes and hollow resonance

Rare near hits explain extreme growth beyond the bulk limit law.

Weak convergence does not control the largest conditioning spikes. One-sided inhomogeneous approximation governs limsupκ2(BN)/N2\limsup\kappa_2(B_N)/N^2 and logarithmic growth exponents.

The hollow shift τ=1\tau=1 is the diagonal β=α\beta=\alpha. It simplifies the residual to

dN=dist((N−1)α,ℤ). d_N=\operatorname{dist}((N-1)\alpha,\mathbb Z).
The arithmetic therefore becomes homogeneous. The formal development also includes Hurwitz’s sharp constant and noble/golden specializations. It does not assert the first Lagrange gap or the converse claim that only noble numbers attain the extremal constant.
