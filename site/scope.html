Scope of the paper

What is covered

  • Exact spectral quantization for finite shifted KMS matrices with 0<a<10<a<1 and physical dimension N≥2N\ge2.
  • The interior parameter triangle, determinant identity and the exact singularity criterion.
  • Rational recurrence, irrational uniqueness, and the resulting 00, 11, or infinitely many singular dimensions.
  • Five conditioning regimes, with nonsingular interior asymptotics and separate band-edge rates.
  • Empirical and exceptional conditioning laws; hollow resonance and homogeneous approximation consequences.
Important limits

What is not claimed

  • The virtual dimension N=1N=1 is an arithmetic extension, not part of the physical matrix family N≥2N\ge2.
  • Interior condition-number results at a singular dimension require the appropriate extended-real interpretation; the finite condition-number asymptotics concern nonsingular dimensions.
  • Interior asymptotic error terms are not uniform as the fixed level approaches either symbol-band edge.
  • An irrational equidistribution limit does not assert independent or identically distributed sampling.
  • The additional homogeneous results do not prove the first Lagrange-spectrum gap, or a converse assertion that only noble numbers achieve the extremal Hurwitz constant.
Author & scholarly context

Credits, citation and reuse

Charlie Dorsey, independent researcher, is the author of Diophantine singularity and conditioning of shifted Kac–Murdock–Szegő matrices. The paper’s references and final scope section give the mathematical and historical attributions.

The formal source uses Lean, Mathlib and established mathematics on KMS spectra, Toeplitz matrices, Chebyshev/continuant methods, Weyl equidistribution, Diophantine approximation and Hurwitz’s theorem. The formal development should be cited alongside the manuscript when reused.

The research manuscript is separately archived as a Zenodo preprint (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23486307); this formalization is its separately citable companion. Software citation metadata and explicit mixed-license terms are supplied: Apache-2.0 for original code and CC BY 4.0 for the paper and explanatory content. The source project’s Mathlib dependencies carry their own licenses.

Formalization and historical priority are different questions. Lean can check a theorem relative to its exact formal statement and foundations; it does not referee the novelty or bibliographic attribution of the manuscript.
